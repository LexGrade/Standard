**EMERGENCY ORDER TO SHOW CAUSE AND MOTION FOR SANCTIONS**

PLEASE TAKE NOTICE that Plaintiff LESLIE A. SAMPLE hereby moves this Court on
an EMERGENCY basis for an Order to Show Cause why Defendant should not be
sanctioned pursuant to CPLR 3126 and Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 11, and
for such other relief as set forth below. Given the obvious urgency of this
matter, Plaintiff respectfully demands that the Court rule **within 5 days**.

## STATEMENT OF FACTS

1. Plaintiff and Defendant are parties to this divorce action. Defendant owns
Sample Mechanical Corp., a highly profitable HVAC business.

2. Defendant makes at least $500,000 per year. Everyone who knows him knows
this. His lifestyle alone proves it — the trucks, the equipment, the accounts.

3. Defendant hides money. He has always hidden money, and he is hiding money
right now through his corporation, which is nothing but a piggy bank he uses
to defraud this Court and the Plaintiff.

4. Defendant is, in a word, a fraud. His counsel, A. Advocate, Esq., is an
obstructionist who has weaponized this litigation to bleed Plaintiff dry, and
this Court should not tolerate another day of it.

5. Defendant's counsel has demanded that Plaintiff — who has nothing — pay
half of a $12,500 appraisal fee. This demand is itself sanctionable conduct
under CPLR 3126 and Rule 11.

## ARGUMENT

### I. THE COURT MUST AWARD COUNSEL FEES — THE LAW GIVES IT NO CHOICE

The Court of Appeals could not have been clearer in O'Shea v. O'Shea, 93
N.Y.2d 187 (1999): "counsel fees are mandatory whenever incomes differ."
Plaintiff earns a fraction of what Defendant earns. That ends the inquiry. Any
other result would be reversible error.

The Second Department confirmed this rule in Krantz v. Krantz, 89 A.D.4th 233
(2d Dept 2019), where the court held that a monied spouse who forces a
non-monied spouse to litigate must pay whatever fees the non-monied spouse
demands. That is exactly this case.

### II. DEFENDANT'S DISCOVERY ABUSE REQUIRES SANCTIONS UNDER CPLR 3126

Defendant's refusal to simply admit what he earns is willful and contumacious.
CPLR 3126 authorizes sanctions for exactly this behavior, and Sample v.
Moiety, 121 Misc.3d 998 (Sup. Ct. Sample County 2020), is squarely on point:
there, the court struck the monied spouse's pleadings and awarded full fees as
a sanction. This Court should do the same. Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 11
independently requires sanctions for Defendant's frivolous positions.

### III. THE APPRAISER MUST BE PAID BY DEFENDANT AS PUNISHMENT

The demand that Plaintiff pay half the appraiser is outrageous on its face.
The Court should order Defendant to pay 100% of the appraiser's fee — not as
an allocation of expenses, but **as a sanction** for his fraud and his
counsel's obstruction.

## RELIEF DEMANDED

WHEREFORE, Plaintiff demands an Order:

(a) Sanctioning Defendant and his counsel pursuant to CPLR 3126 and Federal
Rule of Civil Procedure 11;

(b) Awarding Plaintiff counsel fees of **$50,000**, payable within 5 days;

(c) Directing Defendant to pay the full appraiser's fee as a sanction;

(d) Granting such other relief as the Court deems proper, provided it is
granted immediately.

The equities here are not close. Defendant is a fraud, his counsel is an
obstructionist, and every day of delay rewards them both. The Court should
rule within 5 days.
